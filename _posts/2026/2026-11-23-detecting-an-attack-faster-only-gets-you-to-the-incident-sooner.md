Detecting an attack faster only gets you to the incident sooner.

That's the catch with detect and defend. It assumes something already happened, and your job is to notice fast enough to matter. For a long time that was the whole game, and it still matters.

But the clock changed. AI didn't invent new attacks. It made old ones machine-speed. Frontier models are finding vulnerabilities that sat unnoticed for decades and chaining them together in hours. The exploit can show up before the patch does.

So the job has to move from detect and defend to predict and protect. That starts earlier:

- Know your exposure as well as your alerts.
- Fix what's actually exploitable first.
- Attack yourself with the same AI tools attackers use, before they do.

The old question was how fast can we respond? The new question is what can we fix before anyone has to respond?

Which of those three is your team working on?
