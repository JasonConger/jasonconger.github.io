Cars don't go fast because of big engines. They go fast because of brakes.

C J Low, Field CTO at Splunk, opened our Data Strategy Workshop in Singapore with a great analogy for AI: the balance between speed and safety. He calls it the paradox of safety.

His points:

 ➡️ The business always wants speed, scale and outcomes. Security, governance and risk usually come as an afterthought, and for a long time it has been "a lot more speed than safety."
➡️ Guardrails get seen as an inhibitor, but "it's not really because we've got high-performance engines. It's because we've got the safety mechanisms like brakes and high-performance seatbelts."
➡️ Those guardrails build trust, and trust lets businesses engage with confidence and take more risk.

My takeaway: if we can trust AI, we can actually go faster with AI. Automating the controls is how safety keeps up with the speed of change.
